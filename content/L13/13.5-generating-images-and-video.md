# Generating images and video

Image generation looks like "LLM, but pictures". It isn't. Most image and video generators are diffusion models (or
close relatives), which work differently from next-token prediction, take instructions more loosely, and have a very
different cost profile. They also carry rights questions that text output mostly does not.

## Denoising, not next token

A diffusion model is trained to remove noise. During training, real images get progressively noisier and the model
learns to predict the noise at each step. At generation time it runs the process backwards:

```
pure random noise  →  step 1: remove a bit of noise, guided by the prompt
                   →  step 2: remove a bit more
                   →  ...
                   →  step N: a clean image
```

Modern systems usually do this in a compressed **latent space** rather than on raw pixels, then decode the latent to
an image. The prompt is turned into embeddings by a text encoder, and steers every denoising step. Some newer systems
generate images with autoregressive transformers instead, but the practical controls below are similar.

## What a prompt controls

A prompt sets subject, composition, style, lighting and level of detail. What it does **not** reliably do:

- Exact counts ("seven apples") and precise spatial layouts.
- Long, exact text inside the image (much better than it was, still worth checking).
- Negations ("no people") on models without explicit negative-prompt support.

Write prompts as descriptions of the image you want, not as instructions. "A product photo of a matte black water
bottle on a white background, soft studio lighting, centred" beats "make me a nice bottle picture".

## Seeds, resolution and steps

| Setting | Effect | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Seed | Same prompt + same seed + same settings = same image | Change seed to explore interpretations |
| Resolution / aspect ratio | Framing and detail | Higher resolution costs more time and money |
| Steps | More denoising steps refine detail | Diminishing returns past a model-specific point |
| Guidance scale | How strongly the prompt steers | Too high looks overcooked, too low drifts off prompt |

With an open model through Hugging Face `diffusers`, the seed is explicit:

```python
import torch
from diffusers import AutoPipelineForText2Image

pipe = AutoPipelineForText2Image.from_pretrained(
    "stabilityai/sdxl-turbo", torch_dtype=torch.float16
).to("cuda")

for seed in [1, 2, 3]:
    g = torch.Generator("cuda").manual_seed(seed)
    img = pipe("isometric illustration of a small bakery, pastel colours",
               num_inference_steps=4, guidance_scale=0.0, generator=g).images[0]
    img.save(f"bakery_{seed}.png")
```

Hosted APIs may hide seeds and steps, which makes exact reproduction impossible. Store the prompt, model version and
any settings you can with every accepted asset.

## Editing modes and control

| Task | Mode |
|---|---|
| Generate from nothing | Text-to-image |
| Restyle or vary an existing image | Image-to-image (strength controls how far it drifts) |
| Replace one region (remove a cup, change a shirt) | Inpainting with a mask |
| Extend the canvas | Outpainting |
| Match a brand look | Style or reference-image conditioning, or a small fine-tune (LoRA) |
| Hold a pose or layout | Structural control: pose skeleton, depth map, edge map (ControlNet-style) |

The common mistake is re-prompting from scratch to fix one detail. Inpainting the region keeps everything you already
liked.

## Cost per keeper

Generation looks cheap per image. What matters is cost per **accepted** image:

```
example: $0.04 per image, 1 in 8 accepted after review
→ $0.32 in generation per keeper
+ 3 minutes of designer review per batch of 8 at $60/hour = $3.00
→ ~$3.32 per keeper; review time dominates
```

Video multiplies everything: each second contains many frames that must stay consistent in identity, lighting and
physics. Cost and generation time grow with length and resolution, and the failure modes (morphing hands, objects
appearing and vanishing, inconsistent faces) get more frequent as clips get longer. Short clips stitched together are
usually more controllable than one long generation.

## Provenance and rights

- **Watermarks and content credentials**: many providers embed invisible watermarks or attach C2PA content
  credentials to mark output as AI-generated. Keep them; some platforms and regulations expect disclosure.
- **Training data and licensing**: models differ in what they were trained on and what indemnity, if any, the vendor
  offers.
- **Commercial-use terms**: open-weight model licences vary; some restrict commercial use or require attribution.
- **Likeness and trademarks**: generating real people or brand marks raises legal risk regardless of the tool.

## Try it

Generate one prompt at three seeds and three aspect ratios (square, 16:9, 9:16). Pick the best image, then change one
element (for example, the colour of an object) with inpainting rather than a new prompt.

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

The seeds give noticeably different compositions for the same words, showing that the prompt underdetermines the
image. Aspect ratio changes composition, not just cropping: wide frames add background, tall frames stack elements.
Inpainting changes only the masked area and preserves the rest, while re-prompting would have produced a new image
with different everything. Record seed, model and settings for the keeper.
</details>

## Check yourself

1. Why does re-running the same prompt give different images, and how do you make it repeatable?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Generation starts from random noise. Fixing the seed (with the same model, settings and hardware path) fixes the
starting noise and so reproduces the image.
</details>

2. A marketing team needs the same mascot in the same pose across 20 images. What tools help?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Reference or style conditioning (or a small LoRA trained on the mascot) for identity, structural control such as a
pose skeleton for the pose, fixed seeds, and inpainting for fixes instead of full re-generation.
</details>

3. Why is cost per generated image a misleading metric?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Most candidates are rejected, and human review time per batch is often far larger than generation cost. Cost per
accepted image, including review, is what the business pays.
</details>
