# On-device and edge

Speech and vision are the modalities that most often belong on the user's own device. The models are small enough,
the input (a voice, a face, a document photo) is private, and the network round trip is frequently the slowest part
of the request. The price is quality: device models are smaller and usually quantised.

## Why speech and vision fit devices

| Factor | Speech / vision on device | Big text LLM on device |
|---|---|---|
| Model size | Tens to hundreds of MB (small STT, OCR, detection) | Several GB even when quantised |
| Input privacy | Raw audio, camera frames | Typed text |
| Network cost | Audio and images are large to upload | Text is tiny |
| Latency gain | Large: no upload, no queue | Smaller |

Phones already run speech recognition, OCR (live text in photos) and face detection locally for exactly these reasons.

## Quantisation

Quantisation stores weights with fewer bits:

```
memory ≈ parameters × bytes per weight
Whisper-small (~244M params):  fp32 ≈ 1.0 GB   fp16 ≈ 0.5 GB   int8 ≈ 0.25 GB
a 3B-parameter VLM:            fp16 ≈ 6 GB     4-bit ≈ 1.5-2 GB (plus runtime overhead)
```

Fewer bits mean less memory, less memory bandwidth and usually faster inference. The quality loss is uneven. It shows
up first on the hard tail: rare words and accents in speech, small text and fine detail in vision, multi-step
reasoning in language. Always compare the quantised model with the full-precision one on your own test set, not just
an average benchmark.

Running a quantised STT model locally is a few lines:

```python
from faster_whisper import WhisperModel

model = WhisperModel("small", device="cpu", compute_type="int8")
segments, _ = model.transcribe("note.wav", vad_filter=True)
print(" ".join(s.text for s in segments))
```

On phones the same idea runs through platform runtimes (Core ML, LiteRT/TensorFlow Lite, ONNX Runtime Mobile) or
ports such as whisper.cpp and llama.cpp.

## Hardware limits

- **Memory ceiling**: a phone may give an app only a fraction of its RAM; the OS kills apps that take too much.
- **Thermal throttling**: sustained inference heats the chip, which then slows down. A model that is fast for 10
  seconds may be half as fast after two minutes.
- **Battery**: continuous transcription or camera inference drains it fast. Run only when needed (wake word, button
  press, VAD).
- **Device spread**: performance on a flagship phone says little about a five-year-old budget device. Test on the low
  end of your user base.
- **Model delivery**: a 300 MB model download on first launch is a product decision, not just a technical one.

## What you gain

- **Offline operation**: works on a plane, in a basement, in the field.
- **No per-request cost**: the user's hardware pays.
- **Predictable latency**: no network variance or provider queue.
- **Data stays put**: audio and images never leave the device, which simplifies consent, residency and retention.

## Mix and match

One app can use all three execution places, chosen per step:

```
voice note app
  on device:    VAD + wake word + STT (private, instant, free)
  self-hosted:  summarisation with an open model in your cloud (data in your boundary)
  API:          rare "deep analysis" requests on a frontier model (quality where it matters)
```

Common pattern: do the private, high-volume, latency-sensitive first step on the device, send only the compact result
(text, not audio) to the server, and fall back to the server when the device is too weak or the local model's
confidence is low.

## Try it

Transcribe the same five audio clips with a small int8 Whisper-family model on your laptop and with a hosted STT API.
Record WER against hand-corrected transcripts, wall-clock time per clip, and estimated cost per 1,000 minutes for
each.

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

The hosted API usually has lower WER, especially on accented or noisy clips. The local model is often competitive on
clean audio, costs nothing per minute beyond your hardware, and keeps audio private; its speed depends heavily on your
CPU or GPU. The decision point is whether the WER gap hits the words your product cares about, and what the per-minute
API price adds up to at your volume.
</details>

## Check yourself

1. Why do speech and vision tasks move on-device more readily than general chat?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The useful models are small, the inputs are large to upload and highly private, and latency gains from skipping the
network are big. General-purpose LLMs that match server quality are still much larger.
</details>

2. A quantised model scores the same on a public benchmark as the full one but users complain. What might be
   happening?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Quantisation losses concentrate in the tail (rare words, accents, small text). An average benchmark can hide them;
your users' inputs hit that tail. Test on your own hard cases.
</details>

3. Name two hardware effects that make a short on-device benchmark misleading.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Thermal throttling slows sustained inference after the first minute or two, and battery and memory pressure (the OS
may kill the app) do not show up in a short run. Low-end devices also differ greatly from the test device.
</details>
