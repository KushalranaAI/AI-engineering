# Choosing a multimodal model

Choosing a text model is mostly quality, price per token and latency. Multimodal adds new units of cost (per image,
per minute), new latency expectations (time to first audio), and much stricter privacy duties. The method is the same
as always: write the requirements down, shortlist, and test on your own data.

## Start from inputs and outputs

List every modality that enters and leaves the system, per step:

| Step | In | Out | Example need |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intake | Phone photo of a receipt | JSON fields | Vision + structured output |
| Support call | Live audio | Live audio | Streaming STT + TTS, or speech-to-speech |
| Search | Text query | Ranked images | Joint image-text embeddings |
| Report | Text + charts | PDF | Text model + plotting code, not image generation |

This often splits one "multimodal model" decision into several smaller ones. A narrow specialist (an STT model, a
document OCR service, an embedding model) can beat a general model at its one job for less money.

## Pricing per image and per minute

Bills now have several units:

```
example monthly estimate for a receipts app (illustrative prices)
  20,000 receipts × ~1,500 image tokens         = 30M input tokens   × $0.50/M  = $15
  20,000 × 300 output tokens                    = 6M output tokens   × $2.00/M  = $12
  voice notes: 2,000 × 1 min                    = 2,000 min          × $0.006   = $12
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ≈ $39/month
```

Change one assumption, such as sending receipts at high detail (twice the tokens) or switching to a larger model
(ten times the price), and the estimate moves by an order of magnitude. Price the realistic resolution and duration,
not the provider's example.

## Context and latency by modality

- **Context**: each image eats a resolution-dependent chunk of the window. If a task needs 40 pages at once, check
  whether they fit, and what fraction of the window is left for instructions and output. Providers also cap images
  per request and file sizes.
- **Latency**: a batch document job can take a minute. A voice reply must start in about a second, so judge speech
  options on **time to first audio**, not total processing time. Measure from your region.

## API, self-hosted or managed service

| Option | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|
| General multimodal API | Broad capability, fast to start | Per-unit cost at volume; data leaves your boundary |
| Open models you host (Whisper-family STT, open VLMs, open TTS) | Strong in speech and vision; no per-minute fee; data stays put | GPUs, ops and updates are yours |
| Managed cloud services (speech, vision, document AI on AWS, Azure, Google Cloud) | Tuned for one job, often cheaper and more accurate there; runs inside your cloud account | Narrow; another API to learn; some lock-in |

Volume decides a lot. At a few hours of audio a month, an API is cheapest. At tens of thousands of hours, a
self-hosted STT model on a rented GPU is often far cheaper per minute.

## Privacy is stricter

Voices, faces and scanned IDs are biometric or highly sensitive personal data in many jurisdictions. Before choosing:

- Do you have **consent** to record and process voices or faces?
- Where is the data **processed and stored**, and does that meet residency rules?
- Does the provider **retain** inputs or use them for training? Can you opt out in writing?
- Can you **redact** before sending (blur faces, mask ID numbers, trim audio)?

These can rule out an option before quality is even considered, which pushes some teams to on-device or self-hosted
models.

## Shortlist and test

```python
candidates = ["model_a", "model_b", "managed_ocr_service"]
for c in candidates:
    run = evaluate(c, dataset="my_receipts_100.jsonl")   # your real inputs, labelled
    print(c, run.field_accuracy, run.p50_latency_s, run.cost_per_1k)
```

Use 50-200 real examples that include the ugly ones. Score the metric that matters for the task (field accuracy, WER
on entities, recall at 10), plus latency and cost per thousand requests. Re-run when a provider ships a new version.

## Check yourself

1. Why might a general multimodal model be the wrong pick for high-volume call transcription?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A dedicated STT model (hosted or self-hosted) is usually cheaper per minute, faster, and offers timestamps,
diarization and vocabulary hints. A general model bills audio tokens and may not stream.
</details>

2. What latency metric matters most for a voice product, and why?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Time to first audio after the user stops speaking. Users hear silence directly; total synthesis time matters less if
the reply starts quickly and streams.
</details>

3. Name two privacy questions that apply to faces and voices but often not to plain text.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Whether you have consent for biometric processing, and whether the data can be processed in the provider's region and
excluded from training and long retention. Also whether you can redact (blur, mask) before sending.
</details>
