# Speech to text and text to speech

Most speech features are two conversions around an ordinary LLM: speech to text (STT, also called ASR) on the way in,
text to speech (TTS) on the way out. Both are mature enough to ship, and both fail in specific, testable ways.

## What STT returns

A speech-to-text model such as Whisper gives you raw material, not polished prose:

- A **transcript**, often with punctuation and casing, but with fillers, false starts and mishearings.
- **Segment and word timestamps**, if requested.
- **Confidence signals**: per-segment log-probabilities or per-word confidence, depending on the tool.
- A detected **language**.

```python
from faster_whisper import WhisperModel

model = WhisperModel("small", device="cpu", compute_type="int8")
segments, info = model.transcribe(
    "call.wav",
    word_timestamps=True,
    initial_prompt="Acme Pay, UPI, KYC, refund ticket",   # vocabulary hint
)
print(info.language, info.language_probability)
for s in segments:
    print(f"[{s.start:6.1f}-{s.end:6.1f}] {s.text}  avg_logprob={s.avg_logprob:.2f}")
```

The `initial_prompt` biases the model towards your spelling of names and jargon. Hosted STT APIs usually offer the
same idea as "keywords", "phrases" or a custom vocabulary.

## Batch vs streaming

| | Batch | Streaming |
|---|---|---|
| Input | A complete file | Audio chunks as they arrive |
| Output | Final transcript | Partial text that gets revised, then final segments |
| Accuracy | Higher: sees full context | Slightly lower; partials change |
| Use | Meetings, voicemail, call analytics | Live captions, voice assistants |

Streaming partials are provisional. "I want to cancel" may become "I want to cancel the second order" a moment later.
Do not act irreversibly on a partial.

## Hard audio and diarization

What degrades accuracy in practice: accents, **code-mixed speech** (Hindi and English in the same sentence is common
in Indian call centres and breaks many monolingual models), background noise, phone-line audio and cheap microphones.
Mitigations: pick a model trained on similar data, supply vocabulary hints, set the language explicitly when known,
and clean or denoise audio first.

**Diarization** answers "who spoke when". It is usually a separate model (pyannote is a common open choice) whose
speaker turns you then align with transcript timestamps. It fails on overlapping speech, similar voices and very short
turns, and those errors are independent of transcription errors.

## Word error rate

WER is the standard accuracy metric:

```
WER = (substitutions + deletions + insertions) / words in the reference transcript

reference:  "refund order 4 8 1 2 to my card"   (9 words)
hypothesis: "refund order 4 8 2 to my car"
→ 1 deletion ("1"), 1 substitution ("card"→"car") → WER = 2/9 ≈ 22%
```

Normalise case and punctuation before scoring. Then look at **which** words are wrong: a 5% WER that garbles every
order number is worse for a support bot than 10% spread over filler words. Track an entity-level error rate for the
fields you care about.

## TTS voices and pronunciation

A TTS model (Kokoro is a small open example; every major cloud and model provider has hosted ones) turns text into
sampled audio in a chosen voice.

- **Voice choice**: pick for clarity and fit with your brand; test with your real sentences, not the demo line.
- **Prosody**: pace, pauses and emphasis. Some APIs accept SSML (`<break time="300ms"/>`, `<emphasis>`); newer ones
  take natural-language style instructions. How much control you get varies a lot.
- **Pronunciation**: names, acronyms, numbers and code-mixed text are where synthetic speech embarrasses you. Normalise
  text before synthesis:

```python
import re

def speakable(text: str) -> str:
    text = text.replace("UPI", "U P I").replace("₹", "rupees ")
    text = re.sub(r"\b(\d{4})(\d{4})\b", r"\1 \2", text)   # read long numbers in groups
    return text
```

Use the provider's lexicon or phoneme features for recurring names.

## Streaming and hosting

Stream synthesis sentence by sentence (or chunk by chunk) so the listener hears the first words while the rest is
still being generated. Time to first audio matters more than total synthesis time.

| | Hosted speech APIs | Local / self-hosted models |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | Minutes | Hours; you manage GPUs or CPUs |
| Cost | Per minute or per character | Hardware; near zero marginal |
| Privacy | Audio leaves your boundary | Stays in your environment |
| Quality | Often the best voices and accuracy | Strong open options, varies by language |

## Try it

Record (or find) a five-minute clip that mixes Hindi and English. Transcribe it with a Whisper-family model, with and
without the language set and a vocabulary hint. Then read the transcript back with a TTS voice and note every point
where either step breaks.

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

Typical findings: without hints, English words get transliterated into Devanagari or Hindi phrases get translated or
dropped; setting the language and adding domain terms reduces this. Names and numbers are the most common errors.
TTS mispronounces romanised Hindi, reads digits oddly, and loses the natural switch in intonation between languages.
Compute WER on a hand-corrected minute to quantify it.
</details>

## Check yourself

1. Why should a voice assistant not cancel an order based on a streaming partial transcript?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Partials are provisional and get revised as more audio arrives; the user may still be speaking ("cancel... no, change
it"). Act only on final segments, and confirm irreversible actions.
</details>

2. Two STT models score 6% and 8% WER. Why might the 8% one be better for your use case?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

WER weights every word equally. If the 8% model gets the important entities (numbers, names, product codes) right and
the 6% one gets them wrong, the 8% model is better downstream.
</details>

3. Where does diarization fail, and why is it a separate concern from transcription accuracy?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

On overlapping speech, similar voices and very short turns. It is usually a separate model; a transcript can be word
perfect while every line is attributed to the wrong speaker.
</details>
