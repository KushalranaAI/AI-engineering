# Regions, residency and compliance

On a public API, "where does my data go?" has a vague answer. On a managed platform it has a precise one, and that precision is why compliance teams like platforms. It also means a lot of your design is decided by a region drop-down before you write any code.

## Models live in regions

A cloud platform doesn't offer every model everywhere. A new model typically launches in a few large regions first and reaches others later, if at all. So the order of decisions is often:

```
permitted regions (legal)  →  models available there  →  model choice (evals)
```

not the other way round. If your data must stay in Frankfurt, your shortlist is whatever runs in Frankfurt (or in a routing scope that stays inside the EU).

## Data residency rules

Residency rules come in layers:

| Layer | Example | What it can require |
|-------|---------|---------------------|
| General privacy law | EU GDPR; India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 | Lawful basis, purpose limits, rules for transfers abroad |
| Sector rules | Banking and payments regulators (e.g. the RBI's payment-data storage directive in India), health regulators | Storage or processing inside the country, audit access |
| Customer contracts | An enterprise customer's data processing agreement | Specific regions, sub-processor lists, deletion deadlines |

The sector rule or the contract is often stricter than the law. Find out which applies before you pick a region; this lesson can't give legal advice, and your legal team must read the actual text.

## Get the terms in writing

For each platform and model, write down answers to these questions, with links to the platform's documentation or your contract:

1. **What is logged?** Prompts, outputs, metadata only?
2. **For how long?** Including abuse-monitoring retention by the platform or model provider.
3. **Who can see it?** Your admins only, the cloud provider's staff, the model vendor?
4. **Is it used for training?** Major cloud platforms state that customer prompts and outputs are not used to train the base models, but confirm it for every model and feature you use.
5. **Where is it processed?** One region, or any region in a geography?

```yaml
# data-handling.yaml — keep this next to the code and review it with security
workload: support-assistant
platform: bedrock
primary_region: eu-central-1
models:
  - id: example.chat-model-v1
    processing_scope: "eu geography via cross-region profile"
    provider_training_on_inputs: "no (per platform data protection docs, checked 2026-10)"
invocation_logging: enabled
log_destination: s3://example-ai-logs-eu (SSE-KMS, customer key)
log_retention_days: 30
deletion_on_customer_request: "delete logs + vectors within 30 days"
```

This file is your answer to the auditor and to the Level 8 governance checklist (residency, deletion, third-party processing).

## When the model isn't in your region

The common case: the model your evals prefer isn't in your mandated region. Options, roughly from least to most effort:

1. **Cross-region inference within an allowed geography.** Some platforms let you call a model through a profile that routes across several regions in, say, the EU or the US. If "processed somewhere in the EU" satisfies the rule, this is the simplest fix. Check that your data at rest (logs, indexes) still stays where it must.
2. **Pick a different model** that is available locally, and measure the quality gap honestly on your eval set.
3. **Self-host an open-weight model** in the required region on your own GPUs.
4. **Document an exception**: for example, only non-personal data (public product docs) goes to the out-of-region model, enforced in code. Get it signed off.

## Try it

Your company is an Indian fintech. A new feature summarises customer support chats, which include account numbers. Write the data-handling record for it: which region, which model constraints, logging, retention, and what you'd do if your preferred model isn't offered in an Indian region.

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

Region: an India region of the chosen cloud (e.g. Mumbai), because payment and account data may fall under sector localisation rules; confirm with compliance. Models: only those available in that region, or a routing scope confirmed to stay in India. Logging: invocation logging on, stored in the same region, encrypted with a customer-managed key, retained only as long as policy requires (e.g. 30 days). Redact account numbers before the call where the summary doesn't need them. Training terms: written confirmation that inputs aren't used for training. If the preferred model isn't available: evaluate locally available models first; if none is good enough, self-host an open-weight model in-region; do not route raw chats abroad without a signed exception.
</details>

## Check yourself

1. Why do permitted regions often come before model choice?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Legal and contractual rules fix where data may be processed, and each region only offers some models. The evals then choose among what is available there.
</details>

2. Name four things you should have in writing about how a platform handles your data.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

What is logged, how long it is kept, who can access it, whether it's used for training, and where it is processed (any four).
</details>

3. What should you check before relying on cross-region inference to solve an availability gap?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

That every region it may route to is inside the geography your rules permit, and that stored data such as logs and indexes still stays where it must.
</details>
